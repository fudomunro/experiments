For quite a while, one of my principles as a manager has been to expose myself to whatever frictions my reports were exposed to, rather than optimizing my personal setup to avoid them. The idea being that it would help me priotize sanding off those rough edges. Internal frictions tend to be poorly prioritized, because they don't usually get a Product Manager, and those who feel them worst are often ICs who can implement personal workarounds.

An example of these kinds of workarounds would be making a bunch of shell aliases for overly-complex internal operations. I think it's great when individuals do this, but as a leader, it means you probably have a scaling problem, and you are unlikely to get much insight into it.

But what if we didn't have to prioritize which rough edges we sanded off? What if "AI" gives us effectively unlimited development resources in this area?

What if we use AI to implement solutions that eliminate frictions, that wouldn't previously have been cost effective to eliminate? What if we do it together, building a fabric of "rough AI" that we can overlay onto the complex and intricate interfaces in the world that introduce so much friction?

Like what?

Vendor lock in seems like a pretty good target. Build and maintain libraries that can sync your content to any vendor.
