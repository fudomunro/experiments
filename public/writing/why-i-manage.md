# Why I manage

## Social Difficulty

As a child, I never had much success communicating with other people. I had a stutter, and very little interest in other people. I found interactions baffling and frustrating.

When I first started using computers, I found them so much easier to understand than people. You gave them instructions, and they carried them out. Sometimes incorrectly, but usually that could be debugged and fixed.

So I learned all about making computers do things. Hardware, software, networking, I could tweak whatever was needed to get the results I wanted.

But I could only build so much by myself. At a certain point, I needed to collaborate with others to accomplish what I wanted. So I started to learn how to communicate requirements to other people.

Eventually, I got really good at it. I became a project manager and scrum master, and eventually a Director of Developer Productivity.

But the more work I delegated, the more I became disappointed with the results. Architecting and planning projects was not enough to guarantee their successful completion.

I needed to understand why. Why some teams could take a project and execute it beautifully, while others floundered. So I took management training, and began to explore how people worked together.

I learned that when people don't trust each other, all the well-structured JIRA tickets in the world won't lead them to success. They will spend much more time obtaining and maintaining alignment (if they even do so at all.)

If you look at my resume, I begin it with a philosophical presentation that corresponds to this evolution:
"People > Process > Product"

Originally, I just worked with a computer to produce a Product. Then, I designed Processes for others to use when producing a Production. Now, I nurture People to use Processes to product a Product.

I started using computers to avoid talking to People, and now in order to achieve the results I was looking for, I do nothing but talk to People.
