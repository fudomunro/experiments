I've always had side projects of my own, and for several years I've had a couple of collaborative side projects on the go. An hour or two a week, at most.

When I was laid off in November, it seemed like a good idea to dive into these projects and promote them as a way to improve my portfolio. After all, I created this blog for very similar reasons a dozen years ago, and that seems to have worked.

So I've had several projects on the go, but they've developed much more slowly than I had expected.

Because I've spent much of the last dozen years training myself to delegate work and use that process to train folk. Similar to the what the Zig folk had to say here ____

I'm a manager now, and for a long time, it's seemed clear to me that I could do more helping others than I could ever hope to do myself.

So even when my collaborators have been available, we've focused more on discussing and practices AI development techniques, rather than using those techniques to build things. Because sure I can tell an agent "implement ticket 321," and I can help you set up Claude or whatever to do it too, but you won't learn anything from just that.

"Work" for me involves uplifting others, so there's a tension in developing a portfolio without collaborators.



Since I was laid off, it's been more difficult than expected expanding my portfolio. I initially attributed this to general executive disfunction caused by our current world situation, and indeed that's probably a good part of it. But there's definitely something else at work here.

I do have a half-dozen projects on the go, but all but one of them are collaborative efforts, and generally speaking the only time I make significant progress on my own is in the few hours before I have a scheduled pairing with a collaborator.
It's not a technical limitation, there's not anything particularly complex or novel about any of the implementations, other than we're having LLMs build them for us. When pairings do happen, I'm almost always the one driving.

I got a new perspective on the situation when I read about how Zig is very strict about LLM-generated contributions. https://kristoff.it/blog/contributor-poker-and-ai/

Over the past 15 years as I've transitioned away from IC status, I've trained myself to delegate work and use that process to teach and empower folk. Like Zig, I'm not looking to have someone take work away from me, but rather cultivate that person into a positive contributor.

Especially now where I'm using LLMs to handle most of the implementation, so I'm not even having to learn modern framework conventions.
