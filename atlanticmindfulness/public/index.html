    Secular and Spiritual Mindfulness: Is there a difference?

    Thursday, November 26, 2026, 7:00–8:30 PM · Halifax Central Library, 2nd floor BMO Community Room. Facilitated by David Whitehorn and David Sable.

    Over the past fifty years, the practice of mindfulness, in all its many forms, has become a familiar and respected addition to western cultures. Mindfulness has played a major role in Eastern cultures for several thousand years, and the initial introduction of mindfulness in the west came from the east in the form of spiritual traditions, particularly Buddhism. However, more recently, mindfulness has become widely available in a purely secular context, devoid of any spiritual overlay.

    In this gathering we will use contemplative processes to explore the open question: Is there a difference between mindfulness taught and practiced in a secular, as opposed to a spiritual context?

    We will explore this open question from both a personal and societal perspective, recognizing that mindfulness may play a critical role in helping the human species adapt to the multiple challenges we face in a rapidly changing world.

    David Whitehorn, PhD, MSN is cofounder of the ACC and has been a contemplative practitioner and teacher for over fifty years. David Sable, PhD is also a long-time contemplative practitioner and Adjunct Professor at Saint Mary’s University. He is the author of Mindfulness and Contemplative Interaction for Higher Education: Reflections on Transformative Learning and Connectedness.

02 · About ACC

About the Atlantic Contemplative Centre

Our vision is that the people, organizations, and institutions of Atlantic Canada will have full access to the benefits of contemplative and mindfulness practices.

A little about us

During the 16 years the ACC’s existence, faculty have facilitated over 2,400 open mindfulness groups (150 sessions each year), four healthcare retreats (70 people), four extended practice sessions (40 people), applied mindfulness workshops for a variety of organizations (400 people), compassion training workshops (200 people), compassionate organizational training for the new Halifax Hospice (30 people), individual mentorship program (25 people), two all day mindfulness retreats (20 people), five library lecture series (200 people), and two mindfulness facilitator training programs (25 people); and numerous research projects. The ACC has also hosted five annual conferences (300+ people) and we have 450 members of the Atlantic mindful network.
